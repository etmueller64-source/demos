About us

A Main Street church that's kept its doors open since 1908.

We're small enough to know your name and old enough to have earned a little trust. Here's who we are, what we believe, and who you'll meet on a Sunday.

Our story

118 years is a long time to keep the lights on. Here's the short version.

1908

Twelve founding members start meeting in a small storefront on Main Street, in a building that used to house an undertaker's shop. It's a modest beginning — but the doors are open.

1920s–40s

A young reverend and his wife, a former missionary, take on the little congregation. She starts a missionary society and brings in guest speakers from around the world — and the church's outward-looking streak is born.

1955

The congregation outgrows its original space. A new sanctuary goes up at the corner of Main and Queen, seating around 300 — the same building we gather in today.

Today

Four gatherings a week, three generations under one roof, and the same conviction that started it all: everybody's welcome at the table.

What we believe

One conviction, worn simply.

We believe in the unconditional love of Jesus Christ — not as a slogan, but as the reason the doors stay open every week. We hold to the Wesleyan-Holiness tradition: scripture taken seriously, grace given generously, and a belief that a changed heart shows up in how you treat your neighbours.

We're part of the worldwide Church of the Nazarene family, but this particular building has belonged to Main Street since 1908 — and you don't need to have any of it figured out before you walk in.
